Trends in Health and Access to Care by Sexual Orientation: Evidence from the 2013-2024 National Health Interview Survey

Gilbert Gonzales, Dalton Malmin, Christopher Julian. Submitted.

Abstract

Using 2013–2024 National Health Interview Survey data, we examined changes in health, smoking, psychological distress, and cost-related barriers to care among US adults identifying as gay or lesbian, bisexual, or heterosexual. Disparities persisted across the study period and were especially pronounced among bisexual adults for psychological distress and unmet mental health care.

Which Plans Survive? Product Selection Under Marketplace Menu Caps

Dalton Malmin

Abstract

Beginning in 2024, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services limited the number of non-standardized plans an insurer could offer within narrowly defined Marketplace product cells. I use CMS plan, enrollment, network, and cost-sharing data from 2022–2025 to study which products insurers retain when menus must shrink.

The caps generated nearly one-for-one reductions in exposed cells. Insurers disproportionately protected plans with greater enrollment, while pruning also changed some dimensions of menu variety. The results show how supply-side menu regulation reshapes product selection in health insurance markets.
